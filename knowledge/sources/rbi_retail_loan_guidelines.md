# Retail Loan Benchmarks and Repayment Principles

> **Provenance Notice:** Team-authored educational summary based on public Reserve Bank of India (RBI) retail lending and External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR) concepts; not an official RBI circular or publication.

## External Benchmark Lending Rates (EBLR) Principles
Under RBI framework concepts for commercial banks in India, floating-rate personal, home, and retail loans are linked to external benchmarks (such as the RBI Policy Repo Rate or Treasury bill yields). As the external benchmark adjusts, loan interest rates reset periodically, exposing variable-rate retail borrowers to interest-rate shock risk.

## Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) Principles
The standard amortizing loan formula dictates the monthly EMI:
`EMI = P * r * (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n - 1)`
where `P` is principal, `r` is monthly interest rate (`annual_rate / 12`), and `n` is tenure in months. In edge cases where `r = 0%` (such as interest-free promotional financing), the formula simplifies strictly to `EMI = P / n`.

## Debt Service Affordability and FOIR Concepts
In retail banking practice, institutions evaluate the Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio (FOIR) or Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio. Financial planning heuristics suggest keeping total monthly EMIs below 40% to 50% of verified net monthly take-home income. Exceeding 50% leaves narrow buffers for essential living expenses and unexpected financial shocks.

## Non-Approval Nature of Educational Computations
Deterministic EMI calculators and affordability indicators are informational financial tools. They do not represent loan approval commitments or credit sanction guarantees by regulated financial institutions.
