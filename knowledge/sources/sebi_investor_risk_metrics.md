# Portfolio Risk and Diversification Principles

> **Provenance Notice:** Team-authored educational summary based on Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) investor education concepts; not an official SEBI circular or publication.

## Statistical Measurement of Investment Risk
Investment education literature emphasizes that market risk involves volatility, drawdowns, and tail risk, rather than simply day-to-day fluctuations:
1. Annualized Volatility: Standard deviation of daily returns scaled by the square root of 252 trading days. It measures the dispersion of historical returns around their mean.
2. Maximum Drawdown (MDD): The peak-to-trough percentage decline in portfolio wealth over a specific holding period. It quantifies the worst historical paper loss before recovery.

## Value-at-Risk (VaR) and Tail Risk Disclosures
Value-at-Risk (VaR) estimates the minimum percentage loss expected over a defined horizon (e.g. 1 day) at a specified confidence level (e.g. 95% or 99%). Key risk caveats include:
1. VaR is NOT a maximum loss cap: Losses in the extreme tail (beyond 95% or 99%) can significantly exceed the VaR cutoff.
2. Expected Shortfall (CVaR): Measures the average loss incurred when the VaR threshold is breached. It offers a more coherent measure of tail risk than VaR alone.
3. Parametric vs Historical: Parametric VaR assumes normal return distributions, understating fat-tail market risks. Historical VaR relies solely on realized empirical distributions.

## Multi-Asset Diversification Principles
Correlation between portfolio constituents determines diversification benefits. Imperfectly correlated assets (correlation < 1.0) reduce aggregate portfolio volatility without sacrificing individual asset expected returns.
