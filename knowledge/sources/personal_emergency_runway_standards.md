# Household Emergency Reserves and Cash-Flow Runway

> **Provenance Notice:** Team-authored educational summary based on personal finance planning literature and National Centre for Financial Education (NCFE) concepts; not an official regulatory text.

## Emergency Fund Planning Standards
Household financial resilience literature recommends maintaining a liquid emergency cash reserve to protect against unanticipated income loss:
1. Runway Months Definition: `runway_months = liquid_savings / monthly_essential_outflows`, where essential outflows comprise non-negotiable living costs (rent, utilities, groceries) plus mandatory loan EMIs. Discretionary spending is excluded from survival outflow baselines.
2. Planning Target: Common financial planning heuristics recommend maintaining 3 to 6 months of essential living expenses in liquid savings. Households with variable income or single earners often target 6 to 12 months.

## Emergency Fund Gap Analysis
The emergency fund gap represents the monetary deficit between the household's current liquid reserves and its target planning buffer:
`emergency_fund_gap = max(0.0, (target_runway_months * monthly_essential_outflows) - liquid_savings)`
A positive gap indicates that the household does not meet its target buffer against macroeconomic shocks.

## Stress Testing Scenarios
Household resilience is evaluated by subjecting personal cash flows to deterministic shocks:
1. Income contraction (e.g. 10% to 30% reduction in net monthly earnings).
2. Essential expense surge (e.g. inflation spike or emergency medical drain).
3. Benchmark rate escalation (e.g. hike in variable loan interest rates).
If stressed net monthly surplus falls below zero (`stressed_surplus < 0`), liquid savings are actively depleted.
